What counts as a productive entry?

A carried, dumped, or played Zone Entry followed by at least one same-team Shot or Goal within the selected 10, 20, or 30 seconds. The denominator includes all selected entries, even ones ending before the window expires.

When does a sequence end?

At the first new entry, faceoff, penalty event, goal, period end, opponent event indicating possession, or same-team possession event outside the offensive zone (x < 125). A same-second dump release accompanying a dumped entry is treated as part of that entry. The terminating event appears in the timeline. Incomplete passes alone do not prove a change of possession.

Why can a small Pass sample be misleading?

Pass entries often have fewer examples than carries or dumps. With a small sample, just a few successful or unsuccessful entries can move the percentage substantially. A high Pass shot-generation rate may therefore reflect the particular plays in this dataset rather than a reliable advantage.

Compare the number of entries alongside each percentage. Treat Pass results as a starting point for reviewing plays and gathering more examples, rather than proof that passing is better than carrying or dumping.

What are the limitations?

Event data does not continuously track possession or whistles. These rules infer sequences and can end them early or late. Dump-in recoveries are included when the same team retrieves the puck before opponent possession evidence. The situation filter uses skater counts at entry and does not identify goalie status. Comparisons do not adjust for opponent, player ability, game state, or repeated observations within games.

How is the data prepared?

Raw rows are preserved and validated for clocks, teams, skater counts, and rink coordinates. Events are grouped by date, teams, and period, ordered by descending clock, and tied by original source row. No events are deduplicated: same-second records can describe distinct actions. SQLite stores normalized events; the API derives sequences from these events.
